This paper studies what the 2023 banking shock cost the banks that survived it. Following the banks the equity market marked down in March 2023 through the end of 2025, I show that most regained their pre-shock share price and that their deposit base returned to the level of comparable banks within a quarter, despite the fact that their earning power never returned at all. Net interest margin stands approximately 0.4 points below comparable banks three years later, and has not closed in any quarter since. The gap is on the funding side rather than the asset side: the two groups earn statistically identical returns on their assets throughout the sample, so what separates them is the price they pay for deposits and not the yield they earn on what they hold. The results highlight a lasting cost of deposit funding: a bank can rebuild its deposit base without rebuilding the margin it earned on it.
